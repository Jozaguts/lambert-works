## FAQ Draft Answers

### Can a small drywall hole be repaired without replacing the whole wall?

Often, yes. Small holes, dents, nail pops, and isolated damaged areas can usually be patched and sanded without replacing a full wall. Larger, soft, wet, or repeatedly cracked areas may need closer review before the right repair approach is clear.

### Will the patch still show after painting?

The quality of the finished wall depends on patching, sanding, surface blending, primer, and paint prep. A rushed patch can still show when light hits the wall. The article should set the expectation that LambertWorks focuses on a clean, paint-ready repair, not a magic invisible guarantee.

### Can LambertWorks repair drywall and handle paint touch-ups?

Position carefully:

> LambertWorks can help with drywall patches, sanding, paint prep, and related touch-up work depending on the scope. If the area needs a larger repaint, that should be reviewed as part of the estimate.

### Should I fix a leak before drywall repair?

Yes. If the drywall damage came from an active leak or moisture issue, that source should be addressed before cosmetic repair. Otherwise, the same stain, softness, or damage can return.

### Can I send photos before requesting an estimate?

Yes. Ask for one close-up, one wider room photo, the approximate size of the damaged area, and the town/neighborhood. This helps LambertWorks understand whether the job is a small patch, ceiling repair, larger wall section, or part of a broader repair list.

### Do you handle ceiling drywall patches?

Use conditional phrasing:

> Ceiling drywall patches may be possible depending on size, access, texture, and whether the source of damage has been resolved. Photos help clarify the scope.

### Can drywall repair be grouped with trim, caulking, or other small repairs?

Yes, and this is a strong LambertWorks angle. Many homeowners have drywall patches alongside baseboard gaps, caulking, paint touch-ups, door adjustments, shelving, or basement finishing details. Grouping the work can make the estimate more useful.
