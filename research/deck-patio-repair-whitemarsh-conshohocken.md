## Practical FAQ Draft

### Can a handyman repair deck boards?

Often, yes, if the issue is localized and the framing is sound. If there are signs of structural movement, rot in major supports, ledger problems, or widespread failure, the project may need a deck contractor or structural review first.

### Do loose railings always mean a full replacement?

Not always. Some railings need tightening, hardware, localized repair, or nearby trim work. If the railing is part of a larger failing structure, it should be assessed as a bigger safety issue.

### Can patio cracks be repaired without replacing the whole patio?

Small cracks, chips, edges, and surface wear may be practical repair or touch-up items. Major settling, drainage problems, or large slab failure usually belongs with a concrete specialist.

### What exterior repairs should be handled before heavy-use seasons?

Steps, railings, deck walking surfaces, patio trip points, thresholds, loose trim, and areas where water collects are good candidates to review before spring and summer use.

### What should I send for an exterior repair estimate?

Send wide photos, close-up photos, where the issue is located, whether anything feels loose or soft, and whether the goal is safety, appearance, sale prep, or general maintenance.

### Do you handle major structural deck repairs?

The article should not promise this. Safe answer: LambertWorks can review practical exterior repair items and help identify when the work looks outside a handyman repair scope.
